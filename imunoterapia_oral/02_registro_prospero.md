### Review title
Lower versus higher maintenance doses in oral immunotherapy for IgE-mediated food allergy: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

### Original language title
Dose baixa versus dose padrão na manutenção da imunoterapia oral para alergia alimentar IgE-mediada: revisão sistemática e meta-análise de ensaios clínicos randomizados

### Review question
In children and adults with IgE-mediated food allergy undergoing oral immunotherapy (OIT), is a lower maintenance dose as effective as a higher maintenance dose in achieving desensitization, and does it cause fewer systemic allergic reactions?

### Searches
We will search MEDLINE (via PubMed), LILACS (via the Virtual Health Library, BVS) and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL) from inception to the search date, without language restrictions (studies in English, Portuguese and Spanish will be included; studies in other languages will be translated when feasible). We will search ClinicalTrials.gov for ongoing and completed unpublished trials and screen the reference lists of included studies and relevant reviews. The PubMed search was run on 11 November 2026; searches will be re-run before the final analysis.

### Types of study to be included
Randomized controlled trials (parallel or cluster) that directly compare two or more maintenance doses of oral immunotherapy for the same food. Quasi-randomized and non-randomized comparative studies will be considered only in a sensitivity analysis. Single-arm studies, case series, reviews and conference abstracts without sufficient data will be excluded.

### Condition or domain being studied
IgE-mediated food allergy (e.g., peanut, cow's milk, hen's egg, wheat, tree nuts, sesame).

### Participants/population
Inclusion: children and adults with IgE-mediated food allergy confirmed by oral food challenge, or by a convincing clinical history of immediate reaction plus evidence of specific sensitization (specific IgE or skin prick test).
Exclusion: non-IgE-mediated food allergy (e.g., FPIES, eosinophilic esophagitis as the primary condition) and studies restricted to sublingual or epicutaneous immunotherapy.

### Intervention(s), exposure(s)
Oral immunotherapy with a lower maintenance dose of the food allergen.

### Comparator(s)/control
Oral immunotherapy with a higher (standard) maintenance dose of the same food allergen, within the same trial. Co-interventions (e.g., omalizumab, probiotics) are allowed only if applied equally to both arms.

### Context
Any clinical setting (hospital, outpatient or research setting) in any country.

### Main outcome(s)
1. Desensitization: proportion of participants who tolerate a pre-specified cumulative dose of the food during an oral food challenge at the end of the treatment/maintenance phase.
2. Systemic allergic reactions or anaphylaxis during OIT: proportion of participants with at least one systemic reaction.
Measures of effect: risk ratio (RR) with 95% confidence interval.

### Additional outcome(s)
- Use of epinephrine during OIT
- Discontinuation of OIT due to adverse events
- Sustained unresponsiveness (tolerance after a period of OIT discontinuation)
- Eosinophilic esophagitis or persistent gastrointestinal symptoms
- Any adverse event (dose-related reactions)
- Health-related quality of life
- Change in the eliciting dose (threshold)
Measures of effect: RR (dichotomous outcomes) or mean difference/standardized mean difference (continuous outcomes), with 95% confidence intervals.

### Data extraction (selection and coding)
Records will be imported into Rayyan. Two reviewers (L.C.P. and V.M.C.) will independently screen titles and abstracts and then full texts against the eligibility criteria, with blinding activated; disagreements will be resolved by a third reviewer (L.V.G.). One reviewer (L.C.P.) will extract data using a standardized, piloted spreadsheet, and a second reviewer (V.M.C.) will verify all extracted data. Extracted items: study identification, country, design, setting, population (age, food, baseline threshold, sensitization), OIT protocol (build-up, maintenance doses in mg of protein, duration, co-interventions), outcome definitions and timing, numbers of events and participants in each arm, and funding. Missing data will be requested from study authors.

### Risk of bias (quality) assessment
Two reviewers will independently assess risk of bias using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for randomized trials (RoB 2), for each main outcome; disagreements will be resolved by a third reviewer. Non-randomized studies included in sensitivity analyses will be assessed with ROBINS-I. The certainty of evidence for each main outcome will be graded with the GRADE approach and presented in a Summary of Findings table.

### Strategy for data synthesis
When at least two trials report the same outcome, we will pool risk ratios using a random-effects model (inverse variance, DerSimonian-Laird), with a fixed-effect model as a sensitivity analysis. Heterogeneity will be assessed with the Cochran Q test, the I² statistic, tau² and 95% prediction intervals. Trials with more than two dose arms will be handled by combining or splitting arms so that participants are not counted twice. If meta-analysis is not appropriate, we will perform a structured narrative synthesis following the Synthesis Without Meta-analysis (SWiM) guideline. Small-study effects will be explored with funnel plots and Egger's test if at least 10 trials are included. Analyses will be performed in Python (NumPy, SciPy) [ou R, pacote meta].

### Analysis of subgroups or subsets
- Food (peanut, cow's milk, hen's egg, other)
- Magnitude of dose reduction (lower dose ≤25% vs >25% of the higher dose)
- Age group (preschool children <5 years vs older children/adults)
- Overall risk of bias (low/some concerns vs high)
Sensitivity analyses: excluding trials at high risk of bias; including non-randomized comparative studies; fixed-effect model.
